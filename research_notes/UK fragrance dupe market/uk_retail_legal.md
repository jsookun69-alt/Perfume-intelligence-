# UK Fragrance Dupe Market: Retail Channels, Affiliate Programmes, Market Size and Legal Considerations

Research compiled 1 November 2026. This is research, not legal advice. Affiliate terms change often, and many of the figures below come from third-party aggregators rather than merchants. Before building revenue models on them, check each programme's terms in the network dashboard (Awin, etc.).

## 1. UK retailers, affiliate networks, commission rates and cookie windows

### Takeaway
Most UK fragrance retailers run their affiliate programmes on **Awin**. Published commission rates are low for fragrance, typically **about 1.5% to 7.5%**, with **30-day cookies** where a figure is stated. Escentual is the best-documented programme (7.5%, 30 days). Amazon UK pays **4% on Beauty and 6% on Luxury Beauty**. Several programmes exclude prestige brands (Chanel, Dior, Jo Malone at Boots) or refuse voucher, cashback and PPC affiliates.

Fragrance is not listed as its own category. The page shows no effective date (copyright line © 1996–2025). — [Amazon Associates UK – Standard Commission Income Statement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)

### Inferences
- For an independent dupe-finder website, **Escentual** (7.5%, content-only), **Lookfantastic** and **Amazon UK** (4–6%) look like the most usable designer-side programmes. Boots's 0% on returning customers, plus its exclusion of the very brands people search dupes for (Chanel, Dior, Jo Malone), makes it weak for "original" links.
- Supermarket and high-street dupes (Aldi, Zara, M&S, Next) are useful content but offer little or no affiliate revenue. Aldi perfume is in store only, and I found no evidence of a Zara affiliate programme.
- Because commission is low (about 2–7%) on low-ticket dupe products (about £7–£40), revenue per click on the dupe side will be very small. Volume, or links to the higher-ticket designer original, will matter.

### Takeaway
Some do, mostly through in-house or Shopify-app platforms (UpPromote, ShareASale) rather than the big UK networks. Dossier reportedly pays 5–10% with a 30-day cookie. ALT. Fragrances uses UpPromote. I found no evidence for Oil Perfumery, Perfume Parlour or Fine'ry.

### Cited Findings
- **Dossier (US)**: one source lists 5% per sale with a 30-day cookie via ShareASale ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/dossier/)); another lists 10% with a 30-day cookie ([UpPromote roundup](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-programs/perfume/)). Dossier reportedly had about $60m in US annual sales in 2025 and 120% year-on-year growth as of February 2026. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/dossier/) (secondary)
- **ALT. Fragrances**: uses UpPromote to run its affiliate programme ([UpPromote case study](https://uppromote.com/blog/altfragrances)). It has no open GRIN programme ([GRIN](https://app.grin.ai/brands/altfragrances.com)).
- **Oil Perfumery**: a sister brand of Oakcha. No affiliate details were found. — [Outlook India](https://outlookindia.com/outlookhub/dossier-vs-oakcha-the-ultimate-battle-for-affordable-fragrance-supremac-news-344211)

### Inferences
- US dupe brands (Dossier, ALT.) ship to the UK, but their programmes are US-centric, so a UK app should check that UK sales qualify.

### Gaps
- Perfume Parlour (UK), Fine'ry (Superdrug's dupe range, sold through Superdrug/Awin), Oil Perfumery and Lattafa-brand affiliate programmes were not confirmed.

## 3. UK market size and the dupe segment

### Takeaway
Mintel sized the UK fragrance market at about **£1.74bn (2024)** and forecast it to pass **£2bn by 2029**. Mintel's September 2025 report says **88% of UK fragrance users think scent matters more than brand name**, which is driving dupe interest. I found no reliable primary UK figure for the share of consumers who buy dupes.

### Cited Findings
- UK fragrance market: £1.74bn in 2024, forecast to top £2bn by 2029 (Mintel 2024 report summary, via search snippet). — [Mintel UK Fragrances Market Report 2024](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-report-2024)
- Mintel's September 2025 report (survey of 2,000 UK internet users aged 16+, July 2025) found 88% think scent is more important than brand name. The market is "slowing as consumers cut back". Analyst Shiyan Zering: "Consumers are prioritising scent over brand name, driving interest in dupes." Its table of contents includes "dupe curiosity is converting into strong sales" (figures paywalled). — [Mintel UK Fragrances Market Report 2025](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-report-2025)
- A Mintel analyst has also said dupes "lack credibility" and that education on fragrance notes is key. — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-dynamics) (snippet)
- Circana measured UK prestige *beauty* (not fragrance alone) at +8% in 2025, per a secondary blog ([Scento](https://www.scento.com/blog/uk-fragrance-statistics-2026)). Circana data for December 2024 showed fragrance up 8% in department stores and 12% in other channels ([CEW UK](https://cewuk.co.uk/uk-prestige-beauty-market-thrives-with-double-digit-growth-driven-by-fragrance-in-store-experiences-and-e-commerce)).
- A claim that "50% of UK fragrance buyers have purchased dupes" could not be traced to a primary study. — [freeyourself.com](https://freeyourself.com/blogs/news/luxury-fragrance-statistics) (low reliability)

### Inferences

## 4. Legal risks of "smells like X" comparative marketing (UK)

### Takeaway
The leading UK authority, **L'Oréal v Bellure** (CJEU C-487/07, 2009; [2010] EWCA Civ 535), held that comparison lists saying a cheap scent "smells like" a named luxury perfume are **comparative advertising that presents products as imitations or replicas**, and therefore **infringe registered trade marks**, **even when true and even without confusion**. The rule survives Brexit in **reg. 4(i) of the BPMMR 2008**. **Thatchers v Aldi [2025] EWCA Civ 5** strengthened brand owners' hand against lookalikes. A dupe-finder that names designer marks alongside clones carries real risk, possibly sharpened if it is paid per sale by the clone seller. "Inspired by" phrasing is untested in court.

### Cited Findings
**L'Oréal v Bellure**
- Bellure's comparison lists told retailers which Bellure products "smell like" which L'Oréal perfumes. In June 2009 the CJEU held that such lists are comparative advertising, not mere information. Use of the marks to ride on their reputation is "unfair advantage" even if it does not harm the mark's origin function. — [Court of Appeal judgment [2010] EWCA Civ 535](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2010/535); [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/media-and-ip/loreal-smells-success-in-smell-alike-case/)
- Jacob LJ applied the CJEU ruling and found for L'Oréal "with regret", criticising it as overbroad. — [Cosmetics Design Europe, May 2010](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2010/05/25/L-Oreal-has-won-ongoing-trademark-suit-to-regret-of-British-judge)
- Mishcon de Reya (22 July 2025): even a truthful statement that a product smells like a branded fragrance was held unlawful. Modern dupe brands use "inspired by" and avoid the word "dupe" to reduce risk, but how courts would treat this is uncertain. Scents themselves are effectively unprotectable: there are no UK or EU smell marks post-*Sieckmann*, copyright is unlikely, and reverse engineering by GC-MS is generally lawful. — [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/perfume-ip-protection-and-the-fight-against-dupes)
- EU courts in Spain (Alicante) and the Netherlands (Chanel/Coty) have since condemned smell-alike comparison lists. — [Kluwer Trademark Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/trademark-blog/smell-alike-perfumes-are-back-on-track-the-writing-the-name-of-your-favorite-perfume-strategy-is-still-unlawful/); [World Trademark Review](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/smell-alikes-lessons-chanel-and-cotys-smell-alike-victory)

**Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 (SI 2008/1276), reg. 4**
- Comparative advertising is permitted only if all conditions are met, including: (h) it does not take unfair advantage of a competitor's trade mark reputation; and (i) it "does not present products as imitations or replicas of products bearing a protected trade mark or trade name". Reg. 4(b) now cross-refers to DMCC Act 2024 ss.225–227 (substituted April 2025). — [legislation.gov.uk reg. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/4)

**Trade Marks Act 1994**
- s.10(3) (unfair advantage of a mark with a reputation) is the provision applied in Bellure-type and Thatchers cases. — [Thatchers v Aldi commentary, Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/our-thinking/court-of-appeal-finds-in-favour-of-thatchers-in-aldi-lookalike-dispute/)

**Thatchers Cider v Aldi [2025] EWCA Civ 5 (20 January 2025)**
- The Court of Appeal (Arnold LJ) reversed the IPEC and held that Aldi's cloudy lemon cider took unfair advantage of Thatchers' mark. The case fell "squarely" within L'Oréal v Bellure, and the "remind consumers of" intent mattered. Commentators call it a "seismic shift" against lookalikes. — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/thatchers-triumph-court-of-appeal-copycat-battle); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/our-thinking/court-of-appeal-finds-in-favour-of-thatchers-in-aldi-lookalike-dispute/); [Global Legal Post](https://www.globallegalpost.com/news/watershed-moment-for-lookalike-products-as-aldi-shot-down-at-appeals-court-in-trademark-tussle-with-thatchers-2006317857)
- Caveats: the outcome is fact-specific, and lookalike buyers are rarely deceived ([Carpmaels](https://www.carpmaels.com/is-the-tide-turning-on-lookalike-products/)). Aldi said it intended to appeal; I could not confirm the Supreme Court status.
- CMS reports "Aldi suffers two defeats in two months", i.e. Thatchers plus another lookalike case. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/are-the-tides-turning-on-lookalikes-aldi-suffers-two-defeats-in-two-months-but-the-jury-is-still-out-on-sailing-close-to-the-wind)

**Charlotte Tilbury (Islestarr) v Aldi [2019] EWHC 1473 (Ch)**
- Copyright infringement in a make-up palette's design and debossed powder pattern (summary judgment, 2019, not 2024–25). Relevant to packaging and visual copying, not scent. — [Maucher Jenkins](https://www.maucherjenkins.com/commentary/copyright-charlotte-tilbury-aldi-dispute); [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/powder-power-charlotte-tilbury-succeeds-in-copyrig)

**CAP Code and ASA: affiliate disclosure**
- The CAP Code applies to affiliate marketing on affiliates' own sites, blogs, social media and voucher sites. Content containing affiliate links is advertising and must be obviously identifiable, e.g. "Ad"/"#ad", disclosed upfront. Where a whole page is affiliate content, the label should go in the title. A disclosure that only appears after clicking was ruled insufficient. Both the brand and the affiliate are responsible. — [ASA: Affiliate marketing advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html); [ASA news](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/get-yourself-affiliated-with-the-rules-on-affiliate-marketing.html); [Awin on CAP Code](https://www.awin.com/ie/market-insights/articles/what-the-cap-code-means-for-affiliates); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/new-cap-guidance-on-affiliate-marketing/)
- No specific ASA ruling on "smells like" perfume dupe ads was found. A related ASA ruling concerned misleading perfume pricing. — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/tag/prices/page/3/)

**DMCC Act 2024: fake reviews**
- From 6 April 2025, the Act bans fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews, and publishing reviews in a misleading way. Anyone who publishes reviews must take "reasonable and proportionate steps" to prevent, detect and remove fake ones. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover. CMA208 guidance was issued 4 April 2025, and the CMA began compliance sweeps in July 2025. — [CMA208 Fake reviews guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/10/dmcc-act-2024-and-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews); [Taylor Wessing 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/the-cma-steps-up-its-fight-against-fake-reviews)

### Inferences (not legal advice)
- **Platform vs. seller.** Bellure concerned the seller's own lists. An independent recommender is not the seller, but if it is paid commission by the clone seller, its "X smells like Chanel No. 5" content is arguably advertising "on behalf of" that trader. The CAP Code treats affiliates as advertisers, so the BPMMR/TMA reasoning could plausibly reach it. The safest approach is factual, editorial, user-generated or third-party-sourced comparisons (e.g. notes and accords data, community similarity votes). Avoid the words "copy", "replica", "imitation" and "clone of", avoid designer logos and bottle images on dupe product cards, and avoid pairing a designer mark with a buy-the-clone link in an ad-like way.
- Linking to the **genuine** designer product (e.g. at Escentual) next to the dupe reduces the "free-riding" framing and also earns higher-ticket commission.
- Disclose affiliate links prominently ("Ad / affiliate links: we may earn commission") at page top and near links, not only in a footer.
- If the app hosts user reviews or "similarity ratings", it needs a fake-review policy, reporting tools and moderation to meet the DMCC Act.

### Gaps
- No UK case directly on a third-party dupe-recommendation website or app was found.
- Supreme Court status of Thatchers v Aldi (if any further appeal was made) is unconfirmed.
- No ASA rulings specific to perfume "dupe" claims were found. A direct search of the ASA rulings database is recommended.
- The Lush and Lidl v Tesco ([2024] EWCA Civ 262, Clubcard logo) cases were not researched in detail.

## 5. Counterfeit vs dupe, and official warnings

### Takeaway
A **counterfeit** reproduces the brand's trade mark and packaging, which is a criminal offence (TMA 1994 s.92) and is policed by Trading Standards. A **dupe** has its own branding and merely smells similar, which is legal to sell; the risk lies in how it is marketed (see section 4). UK police and Trading Standards repeatedly warn that fake perfume can contain methanol, urine or cyanide. Most warnings trace back to a 2015 City of London Police campaign.

### Cited Findings
- City of London Police said lab tests found counterfeit perfume can contain cyanide and human urine. — [HuffPost UK](https://huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/fake-perfume-side-effects_uk_574ecc01e4b096898c8dbf46); [Irish Post](https://www.irishpost.com/news/police-warn-valentines-day-shoppers-after-fake-perfumes-containing-urine-rat-droppings-cyanide-seized-164433)
- A 2017 Staffordshire prosecution involved counterfeit perfume with dangerous methanol levels (goods worth £175k if genuine). — [Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2017/09/20/Dangerous-counterfeit-perfume-prosecutions-in-UK); [Securing Industry](https://www.securingindustry.com/cosmetics-and-personal-care/counterfeit-perfume-contained-dangerous-levels-of-methanol-/s106/a5640)
- Staffordshire Trading Standards issued a fake perfume warning in August 2023. — [Trading Standards Journal](https://journal.tradingstandards.uk/online-articles/2023-08-04-staffs-ts-issues-fake-perfume-warning/)
- Councils warn that street, car-boot, pub and online-auction sales of "designer" perfume are likely fakes, and tell people to report them via the Citizens Advice consumer service. — [Bolton Council](https://www.bolton.gov.uk/news/article/646/something-smells-fishy-warning-over-fake-fragrance-scam); [Plymouth City Council](https://www.plymouth.gov.uk/news/make-sure-your-christmas-fragances-arent-fake)
- Lawful dupes use their own brand names and "inspired by" framing; scent itself is not protectable. — [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/perfume-ip-protection-and-the-fight-against-dupes)

### Inferences
- The app should exclude marketplace listings (eBay, TikTok Shop, Amazon third-party sellers) that use designer names or logos as the product title, since those may be counterfeits. It should only recommend dupes that are clearly own-branded, and it could carry a short consumer-safety note on counterfeits.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 UK IPO publication specific to fake perfume was found. The IPO's counterfeit goods research and its "Fake Free" materials could be checked.
