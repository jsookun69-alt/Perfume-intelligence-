# Win UK dupe hunters with verifiable trust

The UK dupe market splits unevenly between the two price bands. **Under £30 there is far too much choice**: Lattafa, Maison Alhambra, Fragrance World, Afnan, Zara and Aldi/Lidl together cover almost every famous designer and niche original, often for £5–£25 per 100ml. **The £40–£100 band is thin and oddly shaped**: most headline clones (Club de Nuit Intense, Khamrah, Asad) actually sell for under £40. The £40–£100 band is filled instead by premium concentrations of those clones, a few US direct-to-consumer houses with poor UK price visibility, designer fragrances used as stand-ins, and entry-level niche bought on discount. The competition is real but beatable. The Sniff Test is the only clearly UK-native incumbent and has the most data (13,207 rated clones mapped to 5,987 originals), but its catalogue is dominated by perfume oils, its similarity method is opaque, and its ratings are thin. Most other finders price in dollars. Community sentiment says the problem worth solving is **trust, not matching**: buyers complain about inflated "smells identical" claims, batch variation, counterfeits of the clones themselves, AI-faked endorsements, and affiliate-driven hype. Two constraints shape what Perfume Intelligence can build. Affiliate commission on a £20 clone is pennies, and UK law (*L'Oréal v Bellure*, reinforced by *Thatchers v Aldi* in 2025) treats "smells like [trade mark]" comparisons by interested parties as trade-mark infringement even when true. The recommended position follows from both: **a UK-first, GBP-priced, editorially independent "alternatives" guide** that publishes its similarity method, splits the opening from the dry-down, flags batches and fakes, and earns most of its affiliate revenue from the higher-priced originals and mid-tier picks, not the £15 clones. One caveat applies throughout: much of the evidence behind this report comes from retailers with an interest, search snippets of pages that blocked fetching, or single anecdotes, and those limits are flagged where they matter.

## Demand is mainstream, but its size rests on paywalled and conflicting numbers

The strongest market signal is attitudinal, not sales data. Mintel's September 2025 UK fragrance report (2,000 adults surveyed) found **88% of UK fragrance users think scent matters more than brand name**. Its analyst said this is "driving interest in dupes", and the report's contents include a section titled "dupe curiosity is converting into strong sales" ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-report-2025)). Mintel sized the overall UK fragrance market at **about £1.74bn in 2024**, forecast to pass £2bn by 2029, with growth slowing as consumers cut back ([Mintel 2024](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-report-2024)). That pairing of a squeezed wallet with brand-indifferent buyers is the commercial case for this pivot.

The penetration figures are much shakier. A Guardian piece reported that **50% of 1,435 UK fragrance buyers had bought a dupe**, though the survey's publisher is unclear ([Guardian via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/perfume-brands-fighting-a-lost-cause-against-cheap-dupes-say-lawyers)). A Statista figure seen only in a search snippet puts UK dupe purchase at 16%. The definitions differ and neither figure can be verified, so the honest range is "somewhere between a sixth and a half of buyers". Global dupe-market estimates of roughly US$2.7–3.1bn growing about 16% a year are analyst estimates, not audited data ([MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/Expert-Market-Research-v4220/Perfume-Dupes-Size-Share-Outlook-41480505)).

Discovery runs through TikTok far more than X. As of February 2024, #Lattafa had about **251M TikTok views** and #arabianperfume 243.5M, and nearly half of Amazon's top-20 bestselling fragrances were Arabian scents ([Beauty Independent](https://orbit.beautyindependent.com/article/contemporary-arabian-fragrances-gen-z-consumers)). Lattafa reportedly became TikTok Shop's top fragrance brand with more than $63M in sales between August 2024 and July 2025. That figure is global and seen only as a snippet ([News Minimalist](https://www.newsminimalist.com/articles/lattafa-becomes-top-fragrance-brand-on-tiktok-shop-e022961f)). By September 2026 Khamrah had become an "original" in its own right, with 117 short videos logged that month ([Syncly](https://syncly.app/social-index/products/lattafa-khamrah-2026-09)). Researchers could not verify the X-specific leads in the brief, including the @gabysiclones account and a "Fashionably London" Zara trend on X. X looks like a secondary channel.

Every finder claims independence, and almost every one is paid by the sellers it ranks.

## Under £30, Arabian clone houses and supermarkets cover nearly every icon

The £0–£30 band has two tiers. **£0–£10 is supermarket own-label**: Aldi Lacura at £5.99/100ml as rotating in-store Specialbuys, Lidl Suddenly at about £5/75ml, and M&S Discover at about £10. **£12–£30 belongs to the Middle Eastern houses and Zara.** Lattafa, Afnan, Maison Alhambra and Fragrance World sell 100ml EDPs for £12–£30 through specialist UK e-tailers such as Royal Scents and Emirates Oud, and increasingly through Superdrug and allbeauty. Armaf, French Avenue and Maison Alhambra sit on the £30 line and drop below it when discounted.

Performance separates the tiers more than smell does. Lidl Suddenly reportedly lasts about 2–4 hours, with Madame Glamour (Coco Mademoiselle) the exception at 8+ hours ([ScentVerdict, snippet](https://scentverdict.com/articles/best-lidl-aldi-perfume-dupes-uk-2026)). Zara's estimated range is 3–7 hours ([AltScent](https://altscent.app/best/zara-perfume-dupes-list/)). Afnan 9pm and some Lattafa scents reportedly last "most of a day". Zara is therefore a "smells alike, doesn't perform alike" option. The most useful quality signal comes from a retailer: Royal Scents grades its 24 Lattafa pairings as **"Type A"** (heavy note overlap, reviewers make the comparison unprompted) or **"Type B"** (same direction, diverges in the dry-down). It also openly says five popular dupe claims, including Khamrah Qahwa and Yara Tous, are not supported ([Royal Scents](https://royalscents.co.uk/lattafa-dupes/)). That is a seller grading its own stock, but it is the most structured similarity evidence in the band.

## Between £40 and £100, buyers pay for concentration, consistency and real retail

The mid-tier finding goes against expectations: **the famous clones are not in it**. CDNIM EDT, Khamrah, Afnan 9pm Rebel (£21.79 best price) and Lattafa Pride Nebras all sell for under £40 ([PriceSpy](https://pricespy.co.uk/product.php?p=14434791)). What fills £40–£100 is four groups. The first is premium concentrations of the same clones, led by the CDNIM Limited Edition at £71.95. The second is Rasasi, Armaf Club de Nuit and French Avenue "premium" lines at £42–£45. The third is US direct-to-consumer houses (Dossier, ALT., Oakcha, Dua), which have weak GBP pricing and customs friction. The fourth is genuine designer or niche products used as alternatives, such as Montblanc Explorer, Ariana Grande Cloud, and discounted Margiela Replica, Montale and Diptyque.

What the extra money buys is mostly **batch consistency, concentration, returns and authenticity**, not proven better materials. Emirates Oud says Armaf's raw materials shift between production runs, describes the Limited Edition as the "most batch-consistent" version that "closes the gap most" to Aventus, and reports a cheaper "EDP" lasting about an hour ([Emirates Oud](https://emiratesoud.co.uk/blogs/news/club-de-nuit-intense-man-edt-vs-edp-vs-extrait-vs-limited-edition)). Emirates Oud is a retailer selling every version, so this is self-interested. Brand quality varies more within this band than between bands: **Dua holds 4.2/5 from 763 Trustpilot reviews** while **Alexandria Fragrances sits around 2.5/5**, with complaints about leaks ([Dua Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/theduabrand.com?page=9); [Alexandria Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/alexandriafragrances.com)). Researchers found no published blind test in this band. "Better than the original" claims almost always mean longer-lasting or louder, not better blended. CDNIM, for example, is said to match or outlast Aventus in the dry-down while losing in the opening.

## Communities trust fragrance-level evidence, not brands or influencers

Reddit's clone community is large (r/fragranceclones has roughly **293k members**) and already does by hand what an app should do automatically: a pinned "start here" thread links to user-maintained clone-to-original spreadsheets ([ScentVerdict directory](https://scentverdict.com/directory/community/r-fragranceclones); [r/fragranceclones mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/fragranceclones/comments/12hlkc5/fragrance_clone_lists_links_start_here)). A small aggregation of 128 Reddit reviews rates Maison Alhambra 4.4/5, Lattafa 4.3 and Armaf 4.2 ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/tools/best-products/fragrance-clone/)). The more telling verdict is qualitative: within any house, "some of them are winners and some are duds" ([r/fragranceclones mirror](https://redlib.belloworld.it/r/fragranceclones/comments/1gi25y8/best_clone_house_overall/lvdl4dp)). Trust has to be built per fragrance, and ideally per batch, not per brand.

The recurring complaints fall into five groups. **Batch variation** is real but often overstated by influencers ([Basenotes](https://basenotes.com/threads/aventus-batch-numbers-part-2.416244/post-4181433)). **Counterfeits of the clones** are a problem too: Lattafa runs its own fake-spotting page and warns that fakes "smell harsh or fade quickly", which are the same symptoms as a weak batch, so buyers cannot tell which they have ([Lattafa](https://lattafa.com/pages/spot-a-fake)). **AI-faked endorsements** have reached the UK: deepfake videos of Molly-Mae and Rihanna sold a £17.99 TikTok Shop perfume, and the buyer quoted said she would check reviews in future ([Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/68603/1/tiktok-shop-molly-mae-favourite-perfume-scammed-ai-celebrity-endorsements)). **TikTok Shop UK counterfeits**: an Observer investigation found 9 of the first 12 perfume results looked fake, though that was in 2023 ([inkl/Guardian](https://inkl.com/news/tiktok-cashing-in-on-sale-of-counterfeit-cosmetics-and-prescription-skin-creams)). Finally, **hype fatigue** with a cultural edge: a Basenotes user noted that Middle Eastern houses get called "filthy clones" while similar European scents get called "inspired takes" ([Basenotes](https://basenotes.com/goto/post?id=6395679)). YouTube reviewers quote "90–95% accurate" figures as if they were standard measures, which they are not ([Recapio summary of Aromatix](https://recapio.com/digest/10-middle-eastern-clones-taking-over-the-game-right-now-by-aromatix)). Mintel's own analyst says dupes "lack credibility" and that education on notes is key ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-fragrances-market-dynamics)). An app that already shows note pyramids is well placed to provide that education.

## Affiliate maths and UK trade-mark law both push toward the originals

**Commission on fragrance is low, and on clones it is tiny.** Amazon UK pays **4% on Beauty and 6% on Luxury Beauty** ([Amazon Associates UK](https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)). **Escentual pays 7.5% with a 30-day cookie** and no longer accepts voucher or cashback affiliates, which suits an editorial site ([Escentual](https://escentual.com/pages/affiliate-programme)). Lookfantastic defaults to 4% for new customers and 2% for existing ones ([UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/lookfantastic/)). Superdrug is listed at about 1.6% ([Lasso](https://getlasso.co/niche/fragrance/)). Boots pays **2% on fragrance for new customers, 0% for returning ones, and excludes Chanel, Dior and Jo Malone** ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/2041), snippet). Those are the very brands people look for dupes of. On a £22.99 Khamrah sold through Amazon, 4% is about **£0.92**. A £100 designer bottle at Escentual's 7.5% earns **£7.50**, roughly eight times as much per conversion. Aldi dupes are sold in store only, and no Zara affiliate programme was found, so some of the most-searched UK dupes earn nothing ([Student Beans](https://www.studentbeans.com/blog/uk/get-popular-perfume-dupes-for-less)). Some clone brands run affiliate programmes: Dossier reportedly pays 5–10% with a 30-day cookie and ALT. uses UpPromote ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/dossier/); [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/blog/altfragrances)). Both are US-centric. Many of these rates come from aggregators rather than merchants and need checking in Awin.

**The legal risk falls on exactly the phrase a dupe finder is built around.** In *L'Oréal v Bellure*, the CJEU and then the Court of Appeal held that lists saying cheap scents "smell like" named luxury perfumes were comparative advertising presenting goods as imitations. That **infringed registered trade marks even though the statements were true and nobody was confused** ([EWCA Civ 535](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2010/535)). The rule survives Brexit in **reg. 4(i) of the BPMMR 2008**, which bars comparative advertising that "presents products as imitations or replicas" of trade-marked goods ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/1276/regulation/4)). In **_Thatchers v Aldi_ [2025] EWCA Civ 5**, the Court of Appeal applied Bellure's unfair-advantage reasoning to a lookalike and gave brand owners more leverage ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/thatchers-triumph-court-of-appeal-copycat-battle)). Mishcon de Reya notes that dupe brands now say "inspired by" and avoid the word "dupe", but no court has tested that wording ([Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/perfume-ip-protection-and-the-fight-against-dupes)). The open question is whether an independent recommender counts as advertising on behalf of a trader. Bellure was about the seller's own lists, and no UK case covers a third-party dupe site. But the **CAP Code treats affiliates as advertisers** and requires upfront "Ad" labelling, not footer disclosure ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html)). An affiliate-paid "X smells like Chanel" card is therefore closer to Bellure than a purely editorial note would be. Separately, the **DMCC Act 2024** has, since April 2025, required anyone publishing reviews to take reasonable steps against fake ones, with CMA fines of up to 10% of global turnover ([CMA208](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf)). Community similarity ratings count as reviews. This is research, not legal advice, and a UK IP solicitor should review the wording before launch.

## How Perfume Intelligence should position and build

The app's current asset, **227 mostly designer and niche originals with note pyramids**, is the half of the database that competitors already have. The missing half is the alternatives, and the opening for the app is **credibility**. The recommended positioning is "**the UK's honest guide to wearing what you love for less**": GBP-first, independent, method-transparent, and built around the scent rather than the trade mark. The concrete moves below follow from the evidence.

| Priority | Recommendation | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seed ~30 high-demand originals with 3–6 graded alternatives each, across both bands.** Start with Aventus, BR540, Angels' Share, Delina, Layton, Tobacco Vanille, Erba Pura, Naxos, Santal 33, Sauvage Elixir, Lost Cherry, Good Girl and Le Male Elixir. Use Royal Scents' Type A/B grades and Emirates Oud labels only as *starting claims*, shown as "commonly marketed as". | Depth beats breadth when The Sniff Test's top original has only 19 alternatives; mappings are contested |
| 2 | **Publish the similarity method, scoring opening, dry-down, longevity and projection separately, never as one blended number.** Show the evidence type behind every pairing (retailer claim / AI estimate / community votes / blind panel). | No verified competitor splits opening and dry-down; clones typically fail in the opening but match in the dry-down |
| 3 | **Show live GBP prices per 100ml as a range across UK sellers, with "last checked" dates.** | Same bottle varies up to about 2x between sellers (Khamrah, Delilah); most rivals price in USD or per ml |
| 4 | **Make the mid-tier a "spend a bit more, get consistency" layer.** Cover premium concentrations (CDNIM LE), designer stand-ins (Explorer, Cloud) and discounted entry niche, with a "real thing on sale" price alert. | The £40–£100 band is under-served and pays far more commission per sale |
| 5 | **Add trust layers: batch and reformulation notes, a fake-risk flag (marketplace sellers, prices that are too low), links to brand code checkers, and a "known-good UK sellers" list.** | Fakes of clones look like bad batches; TikTok Shop counterfeits and deepfakes |
| 6 | **Run small blind-test panels and publish the results as content.** | Which? shows media appetite; no competitor has any |
| 7 | **List samples and decants (e.g. Emirates Oud's £3.99 2ml) as the default call to action for clones.** | Blind buying at £20 is the main way to waste money; samples cut that risk |
| 8 | **Link every original alongside its alternatives (Escentual, Lookfantastic, Amazon Luxury Beauty).** | 8x revenue per conversion and less "free-riding" framing |

On language and compliance, the product should say "**alternatives to**" and "**shares the [amber-woody] accords of**" instead of "smells like", "clone of", "copy" or "replica". It should show designer names as plain text, without logos or bottle shots on alternative cards. It should prefer community and editorial data over the retailer's own claims, put an "Ad / affiliate links" label at the top of every monetised page, and run a written fake-review policy with reporting and moderation for user ratings. It should also exclude marketplace listings that put a designer name in the product title, since those are likely counterfeits. Supermarket and Zara dupes should stay in, even though they earn nothing: they drive search traffic and credibility.

## What this evidence cannot yet support

Several gaps should temper confidence. No traffic data was available for any competitor, so "The Sniff Test is the leader" rests on catalogue size, not audience. ScentVerdict, AltScent, Reddit, X, TikTok and YouTube were largely blocked, so community findings lean on mirrors, aggregator summaries and snippets. UK-specific subreddits such as r/fragrance_uk could not be read at all. Most £0–£30 similarity evidence comes from **retailers grading their own stock** (Royal Scents, Emirates Oud) or from AI estimates (AltScent), and several Aldi sources date from 2022–24 for products that rotate in and out of stock. No independent lab, GC-MS or controlled longevity test was found in either band. Affiliate rates for The Perfume Shop, The Fragrance Shop, Superdrug and Notino are aggregator figures that conflict with each other. Mintel's dupe-penetration data is paywalled, and the 50% vs 16% conflict is unresolved. On the legal side, no case has been decided on a third-party dupe recommender, and the status of any Supreme Court appeal in *Thatchers v Aldi* was not confirmed.

## Conclusion

The winning position is not a bigger clone database. The Sniff Test already has 13,000 pairings and still leaves users reaching for Reddit spreadsheets. What no UK player offers is **an alternatives guide whose evidence a sceptical buyer can inspect**: graded claims, split opening and dry-down scores, GBP price ranges, batch and fake warnings, and blind-test results. Commercial and legal pressures point the same way. Commission on £20 clones barely covers the cost of hosting a page, and paid "smells like" claims are where UK trade-mark law bites hardest. An independent guide that also earns on the originals and the mid-tier is both better paid and better defended than a clone storefront.

One further implication: the £40–£100 band is less a dupe market than a **bridge between clones and the real thing**. Users who outgrow clones (the "clone fatigue" the community describes) are the most valuable audience. Perfume Intelligence already holds designer and niche data, so it can serve that move from a £25 Khamrah to a £72 CDNIM Limited Edition to a discounted bottle of the real fragrance, something a pure clone finder cannot do.
